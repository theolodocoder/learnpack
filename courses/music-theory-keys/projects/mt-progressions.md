# One song in three keys

## Goal

Take one song you know and play it confidently in three different keys, using a number chart instead of memorised chord names. This is exactly what you do when a singer asks for a different key on the day.

## Requirements

- A song you know well (a hymn, worship song, pop song or Afro-pop track). Work it out yourself; don't copy a chart.
- A number chart for the whole song: every section written as numbers (1, 4, 5, 6 ...), with the section order.
- The song played in its original key and in two other keys: one higher and one lower.
- At least one section played with sevenths (maj7, m7, 7), and a 2-5-1 somewhere if the song allows it, for example into the chorus.
- A recording of all three versions (a phone recording is fine).

## Step-by-step plan

1. Choose the song and find its key note by humming and trying bass notes.
2. Work out the chords of each section and write them as numbers.
3. Write the chord names in the original key under the numbers.
4. Pick two new keys: one a whole step or more higher, one a whole step or more lower. Use the number method to write the chord names in each.
5. Practise each version slowly with a metronome, then at the song's real tempo.
6. Add sevenths to one section and try a 2-5-1 leading into the chorus.
7. Record all three versions, back to back.

## Stretch goals

- Add a fourth key with lots of flats, such as Ab or Db.
- Play the last chorus up a half step, as a lift, in one of the versions.
- Sing the melody while you play, so you can hear how each key suits your voice.
