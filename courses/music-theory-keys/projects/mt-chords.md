# A chord dictionary for one key

## Goal

Build your own chord reference for two keys: C major and one other major key you choose (pick the key your church or band plays in most, or a key you produce in). For each key, write out and play every diatonic triad and seventh chord, in root position and two inversions, and record yourself.

By the end you'll have a page you can keep on your music stand, and your hands will know every chord in those keys.

## Requirements

- **Two keys:** C major, plus one major key of your choice (G, F, D, Bb and Eb are all good choices).
- **Triads:** all seven diatonic triads in each key, with their notes and quality (major, minor, diminished).
- **Seventh chords:** all seven diatonic seventh chords in each key (maj7, m7, m7, maj7, 7, m7, m7b5).
- **Positions:** each triad played in root position, first inversion and second inversion.
- **Recording:** audio or video from your phone, a DAW or a keyboard app. Quality doesn't matter; clarity does.

## Step by step

1. **Write the scale.** Write out the major scale of your second key with correct letter names (one letter per degree; F major has Bb, not A#).
2. **Stack the triads.** On each scale note, stack "one, skip one" using only notes from that scale. Write each chord's notes in a table.
3. **Check the gaps.** Count half steps for each triad. The qualities should come out major, minor, minor, major, major, minor, diminished. If one doesn't, recheck the scale.
4. **Add sevenths.** Add the next scale note on top of each triad. Check the gaps against maj7 (4 3 4), m7 (3 4 3), 7 (4 3 3) and m7b5 (3 3 4).
5. **Write the inversions.** For each triad, write the first inversion (third on the bottom) and second inversion (fifth on the bottom).
6. **Play it through.** Slowly play every triad in all three positions, up the keyboard. Then play every seventh chord in root position.
7. **Record.** Record the triads in each key in all three positions, and the seventh chords in root position. Say the chord name before each one, or label it in your notes.
8. **Make it musical.** Pick a short loop, for example 2-5-1 (in C: Dm7 G7 Cmaj7) or 1-4-5-1, and play it in both keys using inversions so your right hand moves as little as possible. Record that too.

## Example layout

```
Key: C major
Degree  Triad   Notes   Gaps   1st inv.   2nd inv.   Seventh   Notes
1       C       C E G   4 3    E G C      G C E      Cmaj7     C E G B
2       Dm      D F A   3 4    F A D      A D F      Dm7       D F A C
...
```

## Stretch goals

- Add a third key, ideally one with flats if your second key had sharps (or the other way round).
- Add the third inversion of every seventh chord.
- Add sus2 and sus4 versions of the 1, 4 and 5 chords, and record a sus4-to-major resolution in each key.
- Play the whole dictionary with the left hand on the root and the right hand on the inversion closest to the previous chord.
