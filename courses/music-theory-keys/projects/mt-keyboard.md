# A labelled keyboard map

## Goal

Make your own map of two octaves of the keyboard, then use it until you no longer need it. By the end you should be able to find any note by name in a couple of seconds and move by half steps and whole steps without counting out loud.

## Requirements

- A keyboard: a real piano or keyboard, or any free piano app or website keyboard.
- Paper and a pen, or a drawing app.
- Optional: your phone to record a short clip.

## Step-by-step plan

1. **Draw two octaves.** Draw 14 white keys starting on C, with the black keys in their groups of two and three. Use the diagrams in "Note names, sharps and flats" as a guide.
2. **Label the white keys.** Write C D E F G A B under each octave. Check that C is left of every group of two black keys and F is left of every group of three.
3. **Label the black keys with both names.** Write the sharp name and the flat name on each one: C#/Db, D#/Eb, F#/Gb, G#/Ab, A#/Bb.
4. **Number one octave.** Write 0 to 11 under the first octave, starting at C = 0.
5. **Mark middle C.** Find the C nearest the centre of your keyboard and mark which octave on your map it matches.
6. **Find all the Cs and Fs.** On your real keyboard, play every C from the bottom to the top, saying "C" each time. Then do the same with every F.
7. **Speed drill.** Have someone call out (or use a random note generator) ten notes, including some sharps and flats. Find each one in under two seconds. Repeat until you get all ten.
8. **Half steps.** Starting on C, play every key up to the next C. Say the name of each key as you play it. There should be twelve moves.
9. **Whole steps.** Starting on C, play whole steps only: C D E F# G# A# C. Then start on Db: Db Eb F G A B Db. Between them, those two runs use all twelve keys.
10. **Log it.** Write down (or record) the half and whole steps you played, so you can compare with later sections.

## Stretch goals

- Label all 88 keys (or every key on your keyboard) and number the octaves, with middle C as C4.
- Play whole steps down from every white key and name each note, using flat names on the way down.
- Time yourself on the speed drill each day for a week and see how fast you get.
