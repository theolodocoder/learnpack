# Key signatures and the circle of fifths

## Why this matters

A choir chart or a lead sheet will tell you the key with a few sharps or flats at the start of each line, and you need to read it in a second. When a guest minister arrives and the band has to jump from G to A or from Eb to F, the circle of fifths tells you which keys are close and how many black keys you are about to play. Producers use the same map to choose keys that blend when they chain tracks or build a mashup.

## What a key signature is

You built major scales with the formula W W H W W W H, and found that each key uses a fixed set of sharps or flats:

```
G major: G  A  B  C  D  E  F#      one sharp:  F#
D major: D  E  F# G  A  B  C#      two sharps: F#, C#
F major: F  G  A  Bb C  D  E       one flat:   Bb
```

A **key signature** is that set of sharps or flats, written once at the start of each line of music so they do not need to be marked on every note. If the key signature has one sharp (F#), every F in the piece is played as F# unless marked otherwise.

For a keyboard player who reads chord charts rather than notation, the key signature is still useful as a count: "two sharps" means F# and C#, and that tells you the key.

## The order of sharps

Sharps are always added in the same order:

```
Order of sharps:  F#  C#  G#  D#  A#  E#  B#
Memory line:      Father Charles Goes Down And Ends Battle
```

A key with two sharps always has F# and C#. A key with three sharps always has F#, C# and G#. You never get a key with only C#.

```
Key   Sharps   Which ones
C     0        -
G     1        F#
D     2        F#  C#
A     3        F#  C#  G#
E     4        F#  C#  G#  D#
B     5        F#  C#  G#  D#  A#
F#    6        F#  C#  G#  D#  A#  E#
C#    7        all seven
```

**Shortcut:** the major key is a **half step above the last sharp**. Last sharp is C#, so the key is D. Last sharp is G#, so the key is A.

## The order of flats

Flats come in the reverse order:

```
Order of flats:  Bb  Eb  Ab  Db  Gb  Cb  Fb
Memory line:     Battle Ends And Down Goes Charles' Father
```

```
Key   Flats   Which ones
F     1       Bb
Bb    2       Bb  Eb
Eb    3       Bb  Eb  Ab
Ab    4       Bb  Eb  Ab  Db
Db    5       Bb  Eb  Ab  Db  Gb
Gb    6       Bb  Eb  Ab  Db  Gb  Cb
Cb    7       all seven
```

**Shortcut:** the major key is the **second-to-last flat**. Flats Bb, Eb, Ab: the second-to-last is Eb, so the key is Eb major. The only one to memorise separately is **F major, one flat (Bb)**.

> **Note:** Relative minors share the major key's signature. One sharp is G major or E minor. Two flats is Bb major or G minor. The music itself (which note feels like home) tells you which.

## The circle of fifths

Look at the sharp keys in order: C, G, D, A, E, B, F#. Each one is **seven half steps (a perfect fifth) above** the last, and each adds one sharp.

Look at the flat keys in order: C, F, Bb, Eb, Ab, Db, Gb. Each one is **five half steps (a perfect fourth) above** the last, which is the same as a fifth below, and each adds one flat.

Put them round a clock and you get the **circle of fifths**:

```
                 C (0)
        F (1b)           G (1#)
   Bb (2b)                    D (2#)
  Eb (3b)                      A (3#)
   Ab (4b)                    E (4#)
        Db (5b)          B (5#)
               F#/Gb (6#/6b)

Clockwise:      up a fifth (+7 half steps), one more sharp or one less flat
Anticlockwise:  up a fourth (+5 half steps), one more flat or one less sharp
```

At the bottom, F# major (six sharps) and Gb major (six flats) are the same keys on the keyboard, spelled two ways. B/Cb and Db/C# overlap in the same way.

Reading it:

```
Next key clockwise after G:      G + 7 half steps = D
Next key clockwise after D:      A
Next key anticlockwise after C:  F
Next key anticlockwise after F:  Bb
```

The relative minors sit inside the circle on the same positions: Am inside C, Em inside G, Bm inside D, Dm inside F, Gm inside Bb, and so on.

## Why the circle is useful

- **Counting accidentals.** Find the key, count steps from C, and you know how many sharps or flats.
- **Neighbour keys.** Keys next to each other share six of their seven notes. G major and D major differ by only one note (C versus C#). That is why moving between neighbours sounds smooth.
- **The 1, 4 and 5.** In any key, the key one step anticlockwise is the 4, and one step clockwise is the 5. In G, the 4 is C and the 5 is D. This is the backbone of the progressions section.
- **Chord movement.** Many progressions move anticlockwise round the circle (for example D to G to C), which is why that direction sounds like "coming home".

> **Try it:** Starting on C, play the root of each key going clockwise: C, G, D, A, E, B, F#. Then go anticlockwise from C: F, Bb, Eb, Ab, Db, Gb. Say the number of sharps or flats as you land on each one.

## Reading a key in practice

When you see a chart:

```
1. Count the sharps or flats at the start.
2. Sharps: half step above the last sharp.  Flats: second-to-last flat.
3. Check the last chord or the home note: is it the major key or its relative minor?
```

Example: a chart has three flats (Bb, Eb, Ab). Second-to-last flat is Eb, so the key is Eb major or its relative, C minor. If the song ends on C minor, it is in C minor.

## Common mistakes

- **Getting the order wrong.** Sharps: F C G D A E B. Flats: the same backwards. A key with one flat has Bb, never Eb on its own.
- **Using the sharp shortcut for flats.** For sharps, go a half step above the last one. For flats, take the second-to-last flat (and remember F has one flat).
- **Going the wrong way round the circle.** Clockwise adds sharps (up a fifth). Anticlockwise adds flats (up a fourth).
- **Forgetting the relative minor.** A key signature names two keys. Check the home note before you decide.
- **Thinking F# and Gb are different keys on the keyboard.** They are the same keys, spelled differently. Choose the spelling the chart or band uses.

## Recap

- A key signature is the set of sharps or flats a key uses, written once at the start of each line.
- Order of sharps: F# C# G# D# A# E# B#. Order of flats: Bb Eb Ab Db Gb Cb Fb.
- Sharp keys: half step above the last sharp. Flat keys: the second-to-last flat (F has one flat).
- The circle of fifths runs C G D A E B F# clockwise and C F Bb Eb Ab Db Gb anticlockwise.
- Neighbouring keys share six notes, and the keys either side of the home key are its 4 and 5.
